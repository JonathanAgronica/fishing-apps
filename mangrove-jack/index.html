    🧠 How the score works

    Low light (≈34% weight)

    Jacks are ambush predators that hunt hardest at dawn, dusk and through the night. Civil/nautical twilight scores highest, night is strong, bright midday is weakest — though heavy cloud cover lifts daytime scores.

    Tide (≈30%)

    Run-in tide builds steadily toward the top; the top of the tide (water up in the mangroves and snags) and the first of the run-out score highest, as does the last of the run-out when bait is flushed out of drains. Dead-low slack and the middle of the run-out score lower. Bigger-range (spring) tides get a small bonus. Tide times come from the Open-Meteo marine model (an offshore grid cell) — use the tide offset to match your creek.

    Solunar (≈20%)

    Major periods: ~2 hours centred on the moon overhead (upper transit) and underfoot (lower transit). Minor periods: ~1 hour centred on moonrise and moonset. Scores taper toward the edges.

    Moon phase (≈6%)

    New and full moons (bigger tides, stronger feeding cues) get a boost; quarter moons are neutral.

    Weather (≈10%)

    Falling barometer and the build-up to storms boost the score; warm humid conditions help; a rapidly rising barometer, strong wind (>25 km/h) and heavy rain are light penalties.

    Water temperature (multiplier)

    Sea-surface temperature scales the whole score: strong above ~24°C, fair 22–24°C, sluggish below ~20°C. If SST is unavailable a seasonal estimate is used (peak Oct–Apr in QLD). Note: estuary water is often warmer than the offshore SST shown.

    📏 Queensland rules reminder

    Mangrove jack in Queensland tidal waters: minimum size 35 cm, possession limit 5 (per Fisheries Queensland, page last updated 25 Sep 2026). Rules change — always check the current limits before you keep a fish: Size and possession limits in tidal waters (qld.gov.au). Also check closed waters, green zones and seasonal closures for other species (e.g. the Oct/Nov coral reef fin fish closures) via the Fisheries Queensland rules or the free Qld Fishing 2.0 app.
